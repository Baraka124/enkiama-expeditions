# ENKIAMA DESIGN BENCHMARK MASTER

**Status:** Canonical design reference  
**Applies to:** enkiama.com public website  
**Rule:** Read this file before redesigning, extending, or visually polishing any public page.

---

## 1. North star

Enkiama should not look like a generic premium safari website.

The target is a site that feels as restrained, confident and image-led as the best luxury travel brands, while doing something more distinctive: **making Tanzania legible as a complete lived country rather than a catalogue of safari products.**

The website should communicate premium quality through:

- proportion;
- image hierarchy;
- route intelligence;
- typography;
- whitespace;
- pacing;
- precise operational language;
- local knowledge;
- and the confidence to leave parts of the interface quiet.

Do not use decoration, dark green, serif typography, gold accents, large headings, or animation as substitutes for substance.

**Primary design test:**  
> Does this make Tanzania feel more immersive, more intelligible and more intelligently composed — or is it only design?

## 2. Benchmark field

The benchmark is deliberately broader than safari operators.

### Restraint / hospitality
- Aman
- Singita
- Six Senses
- One&Only
- Belmond
- Four Seasons

**What to learn:** image confidence, controlled typography, negative space, quiet navigation, proportion, editorial pacing.

### Nature / safari immersion
- Wilderness
- &Beyond
- Asilia
- Nomad Tanzania
- Natural Selection
- Lemala

**What to learn:** landscape-first storytelling, strong photography, ecological context, grounded sense of place.

### Authored travel / journey intelligence
- Black Tomato
- ROAR Africa
- Abercrombie & Kent
- Scott Dunn
- Micato

**What to learn:** journey authorship, strong point of view, confidence, logistical clarity, transition from inspiration to proof.

### Kilimanjaro / expedition clarity
- Abercrombie & Kent
- Thomson Treks
- Tusker Trail
- Ultimate Kilimanjaro

**What to learn:** route logic, acclimatisation, safety, expedition structure, operational confidence.

### Enkiama must do better

Enkiama should combine these strengths but avoid becoming a copy of any one brand.

The differentiator is:

> **Tanzania as a complete lived country, not a sequence of products.**

Mountain, savanna, crater, culture, Rift Valley, lakes, towns, coast, people, food, accommodation and movement should read as one connected world.

## 3. What “premium” means here

Premium is **not**:

- saying “luxury” repeatedly;
- black-and-gold styling;
- excessive serif typography;
- decorative boxes;
- oversized headlines for their own sake;
- cinematic effects everywhere;
- glassmorphism everywhere;
- endless card grids;
- sales language;
- invented scarcity;
- invented biography;
- fictional departures;
- generic “once-in-a-lifetime” travel copy.

Premium **is**:

- a hero image that is allowed to lead;
- copy with a reason to exist;
- excellent cropping;
- restrained transitions;
- clear route and operational logic;
- strong visual silence between chapters;
- imagery that changes the emotional pace;
- consistent image quality;
- exact inclusions, availability and practical detail;
- an interface that feels obvious rather than visibly engineered.

A useful shorthand:

> **Less visibly designed. More inevitable.**

---

## 4. Core storytelling architecture

The homepage should not behave like:

**Hero → destinations → testimonials → CTA**

The preferred Enkiama arc is:

**Hero → Tanzania is larger than safari → Karibu / human hospitality → landscapes and worlds → journey logic → proof from real operated journeys → people → compose / begin**

The visitor should move from:

1. emotion;
2. to geography;
3. to understanding;
4. to evidence;
5. to trust;
6. to action.

Do not ask for conversion before the visitor understands what Enkiama actually does differently.

---

## 5. Visual laws

### 5.1 Photography leads
Photography should normally occupy the strongest visual position.

Typography frames the image. It should not constantly dominate it.

### 5.2 One image field is usually stronger than a collage
Picture-in-picture, floating-image collage, and decorative inset treatments must be used only when the relationship between the images is editorially meaningful.

If an inset looks pasted on, remove it.

### 5.3 Crop is part of the design
Every important image needs a deliberate focal point at:
- mobile;
- tablet;
- laptop;
- desktop.

Do not accept “cover” as a complete design decision.

### 5.4 Avoid repetitive rectangles
Do not solve every section with a bordered card.

Prefer:
- image fields;
- editorial columns;
- route rails;
- quiet dividers;
- asymmetric composition;
- full-width transitions;
- controlled whitespace.

### 5.5 Interaction must earn its place
Use one or two memorable interaction systems rather than making every section interactive.

If native scrolling tells the story better than a carousel, use scrolling.

### 5.6 Motion is secondary
Motion should support entry, pace or orientation.

Avoid:
- autoplay for its own sake;
- repeated reveal delays;
- excessive parallax;
- effects that delay first paint;
- animation that makes a premium page feel slow.

---

## 6. Typography laws

Use scale to create hierarchy, not spectacle.

Large editorial type is appropriate when:
- the line is short;
- the surrounding field is quiet;
- the image or spatial composition can support it.

Do not allow headlines to become narrow vertical towers on desktop.

Never let typography overpower the reason a visitor came to the page.

Microtype should be functional:
- location;
- route;
- season;
- context;
- chapter;
- status.

Avoid fake-editorial labels added only to make the page look designed.

---

## 7. Copy laws

Copy must be:

- precise;
- restrained;
- human;
- locally grounded;
- operationally credible.

Avoid:
- “embark on”;
- “experience the magic”;
- “unforgettable”;
- “curated” as a default adjective;
- “crafted” as a default adjective;
- inflated claims;
- guaranteed wildlife outcomes;
- invented biographies;
- invented quotes;
- fictional seat counts;
- broad criticism of competitors.

Prefer explaining:
- why a route changes;
- why a lodge was chosen;
- why the guide matters;
- why a day starts early;
- why time is left empty;
- why one place is omitted.

## 8. Page-family standard

### Home
**Role:** immersion + differentiation.

Must:
- load fast;
- make the first photograph feel immediate;
- reduce interface friction;
- move naturally through Tanzania;
- use imagery as narrative, not decoration.

Current direction: **strong / continue visual QA.**

### Journeys
**Role:** show how route, pace, people and contrast are composed.

Must:
- feel editorial, not like tour products;
- use real journey logic;
- distinguish operated records from examples;
- avoid collage-like image insets;
- connect naturally to Compose.

Current direction: **strong / image treatment being refined.**

### Destination / landscape pages
Examples: Serengeti, Ngorongoro, Tarangire, Manyara, Ruaha, Nyerere, Mahale, Zanzibar, Stone Town, Arusha, Great Rift, Kilimanjaro.

**Role:** make each place feel structurally different, not like one destination template with a new photograph.

Each page must earn:
- a distinct opening image;
- a distinct narrative rhythm;
- a clear reason to include that place in a journey;
- practical geographic context;
- a route relationship to other places.

Current direction: **requires systematic benchmark pass.**

### Why / How
**Role:** institutional trust.

Must feel quieter than Home or Journeys.

Use:
- evidence;
- operating principles;
- clear process;
- exact commitments.

Do not over-design these pages.

Current direction: **copy foundation strong; visual review remains.**

### Family
**Role:** explain who carries the journey.

Must:
- show roles and expertise;
- protect privacy where appropriate;
- avoid invented personal detail;
- feel human rather than like a corporate staff directory.

Current direction: **editorial redesign in progress / visual QA required.**

### Companions
**Role:** shared-journey logic and opt-in matching.

Must:
- show only real availability;
- never create fictional scarcity;
- make private journey the default;
- explain human review clearly;
- maintain a sophisticated but stable layout.

Current direction: **concept strong; responsive/hero regression QA required.**

### Partners & Properties
**Role:** explain selection logic.

Must communicate:
- fit;
- location;
- reliability;
- service;
- staff treatment;
- family suitability;
- availability;
- trade-offs.

Avoid behaving like a hotel directory.

### Practical / FAQ
**Role:** reduce uncertainty.

These pages should be highly readable and calm.

They should not compete visually with Home/Journeys.

### Begin / Compose
**Role:** conversion.

The visitor should feel that Enkiama has begun listening, not that they opened a lead-generation form.

Use progressive disclosure and clear language.

## 9. Footer / index benchmark

The current Enkiama footer/index direction is one of the strongest parts of the system.

What works:

- four quiet information columns;
- no boxes;
- muted hierarchy;
- wide breathing room;
- small ochre section labels;
- low-contrast link text;
- destination and institutional navigation living together;
- the page closes like an editorial index rather than a marketing footer.

This should be protected.

The footer can function as a **site atlas**:
- Explore;
- Destinations;
- Enkiama;
- Practical.

Do not make it denser unless navigation evidence demands it.

## 10. Media system

The repository contains a substantial image library under `assets/images/`.

Important principle:

> An image existing in the repository is not the same as an image being meaningfully surfaced.

Some images are currently referenced only by header/menu preload systems or secondary code and may never become a visible narrative image on the page.

### Media roles

Every image should have an explicit role:

- **Hero** — first emotional field.
- **Chapter** — major narrative shift.
- **Evidence** — people, camp, lodge, food, route, real operation.
- **Transition** — changes emotional pace.
- **Detail** — small texture or supporting information.
- **Navigation** — menu/index preview only.

Do not reuse a strong image as a hidden navigation preload and assume the media job is complete.

### Current media requiring surfacing audit

Examples in `assets/images/` include:

- `group-fire.jpg`
- `culture-table.jpg`
- `guide-daniel.jpg`
- `guide-joseph.jpg`
- `elder-guide.jpg`
- `fatima-stonetown.jpg`
- `kibosho-family.jpg`
- `camp-arrival.jpg`
- `night-sky.jpg`
- `stonetown.jpg`
- `lodge.jpg`
- `maasai.jpg`

Future work must determine, for each:
1. whether it is real / licensed / suitable for production;
2. where it should appear visibly;
3. what story function it serves;
4. whether its resolution supports that placement;
5. whether it should be replaced later by original Enkiama photography.

Do not surface an image merely because it exists.

## 11. Performance standard

A premium site that feels slow is not premium.

Rules:

- Hero media should request immediately.
- Below-fold media should generally lazy-load.
- Avoid multiple competing image preload systems.
- Avoid large uncompressed originals where a suitable web derivative exists.
- Avoid autonomous carousel timers unless they materially improve comprehension.
- Use `content-visibility` or equivalent carefully on substantial below-fold sections.
- Preserve reduced-motion support.
- Do not delay meaningful content for animation.

Performance work must protect image quality, not simply reduce everything.

---

## 12. Responsive checkpoints

Every major pass must inspect at least:

- ~390 px mobile;
- ~640 px small tablet;
- ~1366 px laptop;
- ~1440 px desktop;
- ~2048 px wide desktop.

Check:

- headline line breaks;
- image focal point;
- image height;
- whitespace;
- nav/header overlap;
- footer density;
- CTA visibility;
- section transition;
- unintended vertical text towers;
- empty or dead visual zones.

---

## 13. Merge / review checklist

Before merging a public-page visual PR, ask:

### Story
- Does this page have a clear narrative role?
- Does it add something different from the page before it?
- Does it explain Tanzania or merely decorate it?

### Image
- Is the strongest photograph given enough space?
- Is the crop deliberate?
- Is image quality sufficient?
- Is any inset/collage treatment weakening the page?
- Are imported assets actually visible where useful?

### UI
- Are there unnecessary cards?
- Is interaction helping?
- Is there enough silence?
- Does mobile remain a story?

### Copy
- Is every claim defensible?
- Is anything rhetorical or generic?
- Is route logic understandable?
- Are practical limitations clear?

### Performance
- Is first paint immediate?
- Are below-fold assets deferred?
- Are motion systems competing?
- Does the page still feel fast?

### Brand
- Could this page belong to another safari company with the logo changed?
- If yes, it is not finished.

## 15. Final rule

The benchmark is not “does this look expensive?”

It is:

> **Does Enkiama feel like the most intelligent, immersive and locally grounded way to understand and compose a journey through Tanzania?**

If a design decision does not help answer that question, remove it.

## 17. Premium signature — Editorial Continuity Bleed

Enkiama may use a controlled **editorial continuity bleed** as a recurring premium signature.

The image should not end as a blunt rectangular object when the composition benefits from continuity. Instead, the photographic field can dissolve into the surrounding background so image and interface read as one visual plane.

### Use it for
- destination and section gateways;
- trust / people / partner chapters;
- editorial transitions;
- circuit or route comparisons;
- moments where atmosphere matters more than UI structure.

### Do not use it for
- every hero;
- dense operational interfaces;
- places where the image itself must remain fully legible;
- sections where the treatment becomes decorative rather than functional.

### Construction
- match the page background to the dark tonal family of the image;
- extend the image beyond the nominal content split;
- use a directional gradient to merge image into background;
- keep the gradient soft enough that the edge cannot be identified;
- preserve the subject and focal point;
- place text in the true negative-space zone;
- on mobile, prefer a vertical merge or detach the copy below the image.

The effect should feel atmospheric and inevitable, not like a filter.
